Equation setup

Each component is an axisymmetric (zonal) dipole, quadrupole or octupole about its own independently chosen axis. Tilt moves that axis away from geographic north; azimuth sets its longitude, measured eastward from 0°. No spin control is needed about a symmetry axis. Examples restore all axes to geographic north; “Reset axes” preserves amplitudes.

Rotating a zonal component introduces nonzero orders in geographic coordinates, but does not span all possible degree-2 or degree-3 shapes. A general quadrupole has five coefficients and can be represented by two axes; a general octupole has seven coefficients. This playground varies the strength and orientation of one axisymmetric shape per degree.

Let a be the reference radius, ρ = r/a, r̂ the geographic radial unit vector, and âₙ the component's unit axis. All coefficients share a fixed reference B₀ > 0. The signed amplitudes are local-axis Gauss coefficients divided by −B₀, not fractions of total field strength.

âₙ = (sin tₙ cos αₙ, cos tₙ, sin tₙ sin αₙ); uₙ = âₙ · r̂
(g₁, g₂, g₃) = −B₀(D, Q, O); Vₙ = a gₙ ρ⁻ⁿ⁻¹ Pₙ(uₙ)
Bₙ = gₙ ρ⁻ⁿ⁻² {[(n+1)Pₙ(uₙ) + uₙP′ₙ(uₙ)]r̂ − P′ₙ(uₙ)âₙ}

V = V₁ + V₂ + V₃ and B = −∇V = B₁ + B₂ + B₃. The Cartesian calculation above drives every view. The formulas below are the axial reference case only: tₙ = 0°, μ = sin λ, c = cos λ and gₙ = gₙ⁰.

Dipole · n = 1

P₁(μ) = μ
V₁ = a g₁⁰ ρ⁻² μ
Bᵣ,₁ = 2 g₁⁰ ρ⁻³ μ
Bₙ,₁ = −g₁⁰ ρ⁻³ c

Quadrupole · n = 2

P₂(μ) = (3 μ² − 1)/2
V₂ = a g₂⁰ ρ⁻³ P₂(μ)
Bᵣ,₂ = 3 g₂⁰ ρ⁻⁴ P₂(μ)
Bₙ,₂ = −3 g₂⁰ ρ⁻⁴ μ c

Octupole · n = 3

P₃(μ) = (5 μ³ − 3 μ)/2
V₃ = a g₃⁰ ρ⁻⁴ P₃(μ)
Bᵣ,₃ = 4 g₃⁰ ρ⁻⁵ P₃(μ)
Bₙ,₃ = −(3/2) g₃⁰ ρ⁻⁵ (5 μ² − 1) c

At the selected latitude and longitude, project the summed vector onto geographic north, east and down. East generally becomes nonzero when axes are tilted. North is positive toward geographic north; down is positive into Earth.

The radial-field lobe glyph samples Bᵣ at r = a and makes its display radius scale with |Bᵣ|. It is a scalar-field glyph, rather than Earth's surface or a field-line trajectory. Surface colors also sample the field at r = a, including in glyph mode.

At r = a: Bₙ/B₀ = c[D + 3 Q μ + (3/2) O (5 μ² − 1)]
At r = a: Bᴅ/B₀ = 2 D μ + (3/2) Q (3 μ² − 1) + 2 O (5 μ³ − 3 μ)
Bᴅ = −Bᵣ; H = √(Bₙ² + Bₑ²); I = atan2(Bᴅ, H)
Declination = atan2(Bₑ, Bₙ), positive east of north

Directions are undefined when |B| ≤ 10⁻⁹ B₀; declination is also undefined when H ≤ 10⁻⁹ B₀. The vector diagram offers north/down and north/east projections. Each shows the projected head-to-tail sum; inclination always uses the full H, including east.

G₂ = Q/D; G₃ = O/D, for D > 0 (local-axis amplitude ratios)
GAD: tan I = 2 tan λ; inferred λ = arctan[(tan I)/2]

The apparent GAD latitude is an inclination-only proxy, λ* = atan2(Bᴅ, 2H). It assumes a normal-polarity geographic GAD. It is suppressed for southward or vertical horizontal directions (Bₙ ≤ 10⁻⁹ B₀), and for a null field. No automatic polarity correction is made. Tilted or nondipole fields violate the GAD assumption, so this is not a geographic latitude estimate.

With all tilts at 0°, at 30°N, D = 1, Q = 0, O = +0.15 gives Bₙ/B₀ = 0.9147 and Bᴅ/B₀ = 0.7375. The octupole contribution is northward and upward here, so it partly cancels the dipole's downward field: I changes from 49.1° to 38.9°.

The latitude chart is a surface slice at the selected longitude. Field lines are integrated in 3D from multiple longitudes. The selected-meridian camera projects true 3D traces onto that meridian; curves can leave the plane. For geographic axial fields only, seeds are restricted to that meridian to show the planar cells. The equatorial camera projects onto the equatorial plane, viewed from geographic north. Neither fixed camera shows a generally planar field-line solution.

Why does the axial octupole show six loops?

“Octupole” labels degree 3. This axial m = 0 field has three field-line cells on either side of a meridional cross-section: six loop regions. Its radial-field map has four alternating latitude zones: two polar caps and two belts. A lobe glyph rotates those caps and belts around the axis. Other m terms have different geometries, including familiar eight-lobed patterns.

For a pure axial octupole, Bᵣ is zero at latitudes 0° and ±50.77°. Bθ is zero at ±26.565°, dividing each meridional half-plane into three field-line cells. Tilting rotates this axisymmetric pattern intact. General degree-3 shapes require additional parameters beyond those offered here.
